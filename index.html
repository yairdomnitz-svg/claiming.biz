How it works

Three steps between a video and a sourced verdict on everything it claims.

1

Read the transcript

Claimifi.biz retrieves the video's caption track and reads it end to end — no watching, no skimming, no relying on the title.

2

Isolate each claim

Names, dates, causes, outcomes, and casualty figures are separated into distinct, checkable statements instead of one vague impression.

3

Check and cite

Every claim gets one of four verdicts, an explanation of the scholarly position, and the specific domains that support it.

Questions

What Claimifi.biz does, what it doesn't, and where it falls short.

How does Claimifi.biz fact-check YouTube history videos?
It retrieves the video's caption track, extracts each distinct historical claim, and evaluates every claim against a fixed list of academic, archival, and fact-checking domains. Each claim receives one of four verdicts along with an explanation and the sources consulted.
Which sources does Claimifi.biz use?
Analysis is constrained to the 32 domains listed above — historical associations, peer-reviewed journal databases, national archives, film and media archives, university presses, and established fact-checking organisations.
Is Claimifi.biz free to use?
Yes, and there is no sign-up. A per-visitor rate limit applies so the service stays available to everyone.
Why did my YouTube link fail?
Most often the video has no caption track, so there is no transcript to analyse. Private, deleted, and region-locked videos also cannot be retrieved. When that happens, enter the video's title instead.
Can I check a video without a transcript?
Yes. Enter the title instead of the URL. The analysis then covers the historical claims a video with that title typically makes, rather than the specific wording of that video.
How accurate is AI fact-checking?
Claimifi.biz is a research aid, not an authority. It is built to surface claims worth checking and point you at the scholarship, but verdicts can be wrong or incomplete. Always confirm important claims against the primary sources cited.
